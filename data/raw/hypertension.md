# Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

## Overview
Hypertension is persistently raised pressure in the arteries. It is often called a "silent killer" because it usually causes no symptoms while it damages the heart, brain, kidneys and blood vessels. It is a leading risk factor for heart attack, stroke and chronic kidney disease.

## Blood Pressure Categories
Blood pressure is written as systolic over diastolic pressure in mmHg. Under the 2017 ACC/AHA guideline, normal is below 120/80 mmHg, elevated is 120 to 129 systolic with diastolic below 80, stage 1 hypertension is 130 to 139 systolic or 80 to 89 diastolic, and stage 2 hypertension is 140/90 mmHg or higher. Many other guidelines, including WHO, define hypertension as 140/90 mmHg or higher. A reading above 180/120 mmHg is a hypertensive crisis.

## Symptoms
Most people with high blood pressure have no symptoms. Very high blood pressure may cause severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, nosebleeds, vision changes or confusion, which need urgent medical attention.

## Risk Factors
Risk factors include high salt intake, obesity, physical inactivity, excess alcohol, tobacco use, stress, older age, family history, diabetes and chronic kidney disease.

## Measurement
Blood pressure should be measured after sitting quietly for five minutes with the back supported, feet flat on the floor and the arm supported at heart level. Diagnosis should rely on several readings on different days, and home or ambulatory monitoring helps rule out white-coat hypertension.

## Management
Lifestyle measures lower blood pressure for most people: reducing salt to less than 5 grams per day, following the DASH eating pattern rich in fruits, vegetables and low-fat dairy, regular aerobic exercise, weight loss, limiting alcohol and quitting smoking. Common medicine classes are ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), calcium channel blockers and thiazide diuretics. Medicines should not be stopped without talking to a doctor.
