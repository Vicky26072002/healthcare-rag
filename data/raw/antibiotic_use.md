# Antibiotics and Antimicrobial Resistance

## Overview
Antibiotics are medicines that treat infections caused by bacteria. They do not work against viruses, so they do not help the common cold, influenza, most sore throats or most cases of acute bronchitis.

## Antimicrobial Resistance
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) happens when bacteria change so that medicines no longer work against them. Overuse and misuse of antibiotics in people and animals speeds up resistance. Resistant infections are harder to treat, last longer and increase the risk of death. The WHO lists AMR among the top global public health threats.

## Responsible Use
Take antibiotics only when prescribed by a qualified health professional. Complete the course as directed, do not share antibiotics or use leftover medicines, and do not demand antibiotics when a doctor says they are not needed. Buying antibiotics without a prescription contributes to resistance.

## Side Effects
Common side effects include diarrhoea, nausea and rash. Antibiotics can disturb normal gut bacteria and sometimes lead to Clostridioides difficile infection. Allergic reactions, especially to penicillin, can be serious; swelling of the face or throat and difficulty breathing need emergency care.
