# Dengue Fever

## Overview
Dengue is a viral infection spread by the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, mainly Aedes aegypti, which bite during the day. It is common in tropical and subtropical regions including India, especially during and after the monsoon. There are four dengue virus serotypes; a second infection with a different serotype increases the risk of severe dengue.

## Symptoms
Symptoms usually begin 4 to 10 days after the bite and include high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain (giving the name "breakbone fever"), nausea, vomiting, swollen glands and rash. Many infections are mild or have no symptoms.

## Warning Signs
Warning signs of severe dengue usually appear around the time the fever goes down, typically 3 to 7 days after onset. They include severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding gums or nose, blood in vomit or stool, rapid breathing, fatigue, restlessness and a falling platelet count with rising haematocrit. Anyone with warning signs needs immediate hospital care.

## Diagnosis
Dengue is confirmed by blood tests: NS1 antigen or RT-PCR in the first days of illness, and IgM antibody tests after about five days. A complete blood count is used to monitor platelet count and haematocrit.

## Treatment
There is no specific antiviral medicine for dengue. Care is supportive: rest, plenty of oral fluids such as water, oral rehydration solution and soups, and paracetamol for fever and pain. Aspirin and NSAIDs such as ibuprofen should be avoided because they increase the risk of bleeding. Severe dengue requires hospital care with careful intravenous fluid management.

## Prevention
Prevention focuses on mosquito control: remove standing water from containers, coolers, flower pots and tyres, use mosquito repellent, wear long sleeves, and use window screens and bed nets. Dengue vaccines are available in some countries for specific age groups.
